The AP Italian Language and Culture exam is on **Monday, May 3, 2027**, and it is not
the same exam students sat last year. The College Board has redesigned the AP world
language exams for the 2026-2027 school year. Our preparation program is built around
that redesign, and it meets in person.

## Why in-person preparation matters for this exam {#why-in-person}

Online preparation is convenient. For this particular exam, in person is more
effective, and the redesign has widened that gap.

- **Speaking is now 35% of the score, and it is live.** The exam opens with a
  3-minute spoken presentation, then four unscripted questions with 40 seconds to
  answer each. That is a performance under pressure. Students rehearse it standing in
  front of a teacher and a real audience of peers, and get corrected on pronunciation,
  hesitation, and register in the moment, not through a screen a beat later.
- **The exam demands stamina.** Roughly two and a half hours of sustained
  concentration, with no pausing. Our 3 and 4-hour Saturday blocks build that
  endurance. Short weekly sessions, however frequent, never do.
- **Full simulations need one uninterrupted sitting.** A complete timed practice exam,
  properly proctored, is only practical in a room. Students do several of them here,
  under conditions that match the real thing.
- **Exam day is itself an in-person, supervised, on-screen event.** The 2027 exam is
  digital, taken in the Bluebook app, in a room, under a proctor. Practising in a
  classroom on a computer replicates that more closely than practising alone at home.
- **Feedback is immediate and individual.** Essays and project drafts are corrected
  side by side, with the student, while the reasoning is still fresh.
- **The group is the resource.** Students hear each other's presentations, field each
  other's questions, and learn from mistakes that are not their own. The project Q&A
  in particular needs a live, slightly unpredictable audience.

## Who should enroll {#who-should-enroll}

This class is targeted at students that have studied Italian at least for 3 years and
can sustain simple conversations, or students that already speak Italian.

## What changes in the 2027 exam {#whats-new-2027}

- The exam is taken **fully digitally** in the Bluebook app.
- A new **Personalized Project Reference (PPR)** is submitted through the AP Digital
  Portfolio before exam day. The project topic is released in January 2027, and the
  PPR must be submitted as final by April 30, 2027.
- The first two speaking tasks are built on that project: a 3-minute presentation
  (20% of the score), then four pre-recorded questions with 40 seconds to answer each
  (15%).
- The free-response section now comes **first** (65 to 70 minutes, including a
  55-minute argumentative essay), followed by 55 listening and reading multiple-choice
  questions (80 minutes).

Our sessions run from January through May precisely so that students begin the project
the moment the topic is released, and rehearse the presentation and the spontaneous
Q&A well before the April 30 deadline.

## What we will cover {#what-we-cover}

We work with authentic materials, real Italian content created for native speakers
(news articles, podcasts, interviews, ads, short videos). This builds the skills the
AP exam measures: understanding real-world language and responding appropriately.

- **Listening:** practice with native audio and video to improve comprehension.
- **Reading:** work with current articles and texts to enhance understanding.
- **Writing:** assignments to develop the argumentative essay and free-response skills.
- **Speaking:** activities to improve conversational Italian and oral presentation.
- **Project:** guidance on research, the Personalized Project Reference, the
  presentation, and the unscripted Q&A that follows it.
- **Digital format:** practice with the Bluebook interface, so exam day holds no
  surprises.
- **Full simulations:** complete timed exams with individual feedback.
